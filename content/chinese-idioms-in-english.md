Chinese idioms (成语, chéngyǔ) are four-character time capsules. Each one contains a story, a moral, and a history lesson. When translated literally into English, they become something else entirely: poetry, comedy, and accidental philosophy.

This is not "bad translation." This is **Chinglish at its most artistic** — the moment when Chinese metaphor refuses to become English prose, and instead insists on being seen on its own terms.
