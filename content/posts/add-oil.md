## 📖 Origin Story
"Add oil" is a shining example of Chinglish making its way onto the global stage. It is a literal translation of the Chinese phrase "加油" (jiā yóu), which means "to inject fuel" or "step on the gas." The English translation first gained popularity in Hong Kong and Macau, frequently shouted as a cheer to encourage athletes at sports events. 

In 2018, the **Oxford English Dictionary (OED)** officially added "add oil" to its lexicon, recognizing its widespread use across the globe, particularly within Asian English-speaking communities. It is officially categorized under Hong Kong English.

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
In traditional standard English, when you want to encourage someone, you would typically say "Go for it," "You've got this," or "Keep it up." 

While using "add" and "oil" together as a motivational cheer doesn't make sense grammatically in traditional English, the visual imagery is incredibly powerful—it’s like pumping fuel into an engine to keep it running. Because of its vivid and energetic nature, it has been embraced by many non-native Chinese speakers. It is a brilliant example of perfectly transferring the emotional weight of a Chinese phrase into English.
