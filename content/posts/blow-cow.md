## 📖 Origin Story
"Blow cow" (or the alternative variant "talk big cow") is the literal translation of the popular Chinese slang term "吹牛" (chuī niú). Historically, the phrase originates from northern China, where herdsmen used inflated cow skins to construct rafts for crossing rushing rivers. Because inflating an entire cow skin requires an immense amount of lung power—making it practically impossible or a tall tale—people began using "blowing up a cow skin" as a metaphor for exaggeration and boasting.

When rendered literally into English, it transforms a folk tradition into a visually baffling and deeply hilarious physical action.

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
In traditional standard English, when someone is exaggerating their achievements or telling tall tales, you would say they are "bragging," "boasting," "talking big," or using a slightly more vulgar colloquialism. 

When translated into Chinglish, however, "blowing a cow" or "talking big cow" adds an unmatched layer of comedic absurdity. To a native English speaker, the mental image of someone trying to inflate a bovine animal with their breath is as ridiculous as it is unforgettable. It is an absolute top-tier slang expression for any humorous language dictionary.
