## 📖 Origin Story

The metaphor is agricultural and merciless. Chives are the perfect crop for the comparison: you do not uproot them. You cut them — cleanly, efficiently, at the base — and then you wait, because chives grow back. Cut them again. Wait. Cut them again. The plant survives every harvest. The plant *must* survive every harvest, or the cutting has to stop.

Chinese internet applied this to retail investors around 2015, during the great stock market boom and bust. Small, inexperienced investors — the newcomers entering the market on a rumor and a dream — were the chives. Institutional money, insider traders, and scam artists were the gardeners. The chives were cut in every crash, grew back with the next bull market, and were cut again. The phrase spread from stocks to online courses, gym memberships, gacha games, and any business model built on the cyclical optimism of people who have not learned yet.

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage

English has "sheep to the slaughter," but slaughter is one-time. "Cut chives" is *recurring revenue*. That is the satirical genius of the phrase: it accuses not just the cutter but the system — a market where the same victims regenerate automatically, where hope itself is the renewable resource being farmed. The chives are not stupid. The chives are just chives. The soil is fertile, the sun is free, and the knife has excellent branding.

The Chinglish translation preserves the farmer's calm. "We got fleeced" sounds chaotic, like a crime scene. "We were cut chives" sounds like a Tuesday on the farm — routine, scheduled, with the next harvest already penciled in. It is the most quietly furious phrase in the Chinese internet's economic vocabulary.
