## 📖 Origin Story
"急急如律令" (jí jí rú lǜ lìng) is a highly famous ancient incantation used by Chinese Taoist priests and exorcists. Often seen in historical fantasy dramas or zombie movies, it is shouted at the end of a spell to command spirits or magic to execute an order *immediately and strictly according to the law*. 

The Chinglish translation completely abandons the historical gravity of the phrase:
* **"Fast fast":** A literal, reduplicated translation of "急急" (urgent/hurry up).
* **"Biu biu":** A highly popular Chinese internet onomatopoeia for the sound of a laser gun shooting, a magic wand zapping, or something flying away at light speed.

## 😂 Why It’s Hilarious
It takes an ancient, solemn religious command—used for battling demons and calling upon the gods—and turns it into a goofy, sci-fi sound effect. 

Instead of an imposing Taoist priest commanding the forces of nature, "Fast fast as biu biu" sounds like a toddler playing with a plastic laser blaster. The sheer absurdity of mixing ancient Chinese mysticism with the cartoonish "biu biu" sound makes it an instant 5-star comedy masterpiece.

## ✍️ Usage in Context
Modern netizens use this playfully when they are demanding someone to do something instantly, or when they are pretending to "cast a spell" to bring good luck or banish bad vibes. 

**Example Conversation:**
* **A:** "I'm so hungry, when is the food delivery arriving?"
* **B:** *(Waves hands like casting a spell)* "May the delivery guy fly here on a rocket. Fast fast as biu biu!"
