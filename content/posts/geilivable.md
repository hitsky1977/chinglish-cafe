## 📖 Origin Story
Around 2010, the phrase "给力" (gěi lì), which literally means "to give power," became the hottest slang in China to describe something cool, supportive, or exceeding expectations. 

Once again, netizens flexed their bilingual creativity. They took the pinyin "geili," added the English adjective suffix "-able" to create "geilivable," and even used the negative prefix "un-" to create its antonym, "ungelivable."

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
This word behaves exactly like a standard English adjective. The fact that Chinese internet users intuitively understand English prefixes (un-) and suffixes (-able) and apply them to their native slang demonstrates a fascinating, playful command of globalized language.
