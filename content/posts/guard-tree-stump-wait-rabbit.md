## 📖 Origin Story

During the Warring States period, a farmer was working in his field when a rabbit came sprinting through the crops. The rabbit was moving so fast that it failed to notice a tree stump in its path. It collided headfirst with the stump, broke its neck, and died instantly. The farmer, delighted by this unexpected windfall, took the rabbit home and cooked it for dinner.

The next morning, the farmer did not go to work. He went to the tree stump and sat down. He waited. He waited all day. No rabbit came. He returned the next morning, and the next, and the next. Eventually, his crops withered from neglect, his family went hungry, and he became a cautionary tale — the man who abandoned productive labor to wait for fortune to repeat itself.

The story does not end with a second rabbit. There is no second rabbit. That is the entire point.

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage

English speakers say "lightning never strikes twice" as a warning, or "waiting for a windfall" as an observation. Neither captures the specific pathology of the farmer's decision. He was not greedy; he was lazy. He was not optimistic; he was stubborn. He looked at a random accident and saw a business model.

"Guard tree stump wait rabbit" preserves the exact physical posture of the original image: a grown human, armed with tools and knowledge, sitting motionless beside a piece of dead wood, hoping that dinner will deliver itself through high-velocity cranial trauma. It is not a metaphor for hope. It is a metaphor for the complete collapse of rational planning.

The Chinglish version also introduces a grammatical joy. "Guard tree stump wait rabbit" has no subjects, no objects, and no conjunctions. It is a to-do list written by someone who has forgotten how sentences work. The absence of grammar somehow makes the image more vivid — as if the farmer's refusal to think has infected the language itself.

## ✍️ Usage in Context

Modern Chinese deploy 守株待兔 against anyone who has confused a single success with a sustainable strategy: investors who bought Bitcoin once and now wait for the next crash, job seekers who mailed one résumé and wait by the phone, or content creators who had one viral video and now expect the algorithm to deliver forever.

* **A:** "I got a huge tip from one customer last month. Two hundred dollars."
* **B:** "That's amazing."
* **A:** "So I quit my other job. Now I just work that one shift, at that one restaurant, waiting for the same guy to come back."
* **B:** "How long has it been?"
* **A:** "Six weeks."
* **B:** "You're guarding a tree stump."
* **A:** "The stump was good to me."
* **B:** "The stump was a coincidence. The rabbit was concussed. You are waiting for brain trauma to become a business model."
