## 📖 Origin Story
If you ask a native English speaker, they probably wouldn't even realize this is a "foreign" phrase. **"Long time no see" is arguably the most successful piece of Chinglish in history.**

It is a word-for-word translation of the Chinese greeting "好 (long) 久 (time) 不 (no) 见 (see)." Often classified as historical Pidgin English, this expression was brought to the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries by early Chinese immigrants and laborers. Because it is so wonderfully concise and catchy, it quickly assimilated into the daily conversations of native English speakers.

---

## 🤓 Grammar & Analysis
From the perspective of strict, traditional English grammar, this sentence is completely invalid—it is missing both a subject and a conjugated verb. A grammatically correct alternative would be, "I have not seen you for a long time."

However, language is a living thing. "Long time no see" is now officially recognized by all major English dictionaries as a **standard informal greeting**. It stands as the ultimate, most seamless example of Chinese culture "reverse-exporting" a linguistic habit into the English language.
