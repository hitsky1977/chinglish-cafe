## 📖 Origin Story
"No three no four" is the literal translation of the Chinese idiom "不三不四" (bù sān bù sì), which literally means "neither three nor four." Historically, the phrase has several folk explanations, ranging from ancient descriptions of bizarre mythological entities to old structural classifications. Over time, it evolved to describe anything or anyone improper, irregular, shady, or of dubious moral character—someone who just doesn't fit into proper social norms.

When translated literally word-for-word into English, it transforms a judgment of someone's sketchy behavior into a mysterious numerical rejection.

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
In traditional standard English, when you want to describe someone who looks suspicious, untrustworthy, or unsavory, you would call them a "shady character," describe them as "dubious," or say they look "sketchy." 

When rendered into Chinglish, however, "No three no four" sounds less like a social warning and more like a bizarre digital error or a failed countdown sequence. To a native English speaker, telling someone "Don't hang out with those no three no four guys" sounds like a glitch in a math textbook rather than a neighborhood watch alert. It is a hilarious and quintessential piece of numerical Chinglish!
