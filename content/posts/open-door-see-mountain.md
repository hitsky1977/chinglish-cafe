## 📖 Origin Story
"Open door see mountain" is the literal translation of the classic Chinese idiom "开门见山" (kāi mén jiàn shān). Historically rooted in traditional Chinese landscape architecture and ancient literary criticism (such as Lu Ji's *Wen Fu*), it metaphorically describes writing or speaking that goes straight to the core subject without any beating around the bush—just like flinging open your front door and immediately coming face-to-face with a massive mountain.

Over time, it became a standard phrase in Chinese to praise clear, direct communication that wastes no time with polite preambles or fluff.

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
In traditional standard English, when you want someone to stop hesitating and speak directly, you tell them to "cut to the chase," "get straight to the point," or "stop beating around the bush." 

When translated literally into Chinglish, "open door see mountain" transforms a linguistic metaphor into a wonderfully graphic and spatial mental image. It sounds like a prompt from a fantasy video game where unlocking a door immediately reveals a boss arena. Its vivid nature makes it a delightful and surprisingly intuitive phrase for anyone learning how to communicate more directly.
