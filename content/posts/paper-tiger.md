## 📖 Origin Story
"Paper tiger" is an ancient Chinese idiom, but its explosive popularity in the Western world is entirely credited to Chairman Mao Zedong. In a famous 1946 interview with American journalist Anna Louise Strong, Mao used the term "纸老虎" (zhǐ lǎo hǔ) to describe imperialists and reactionaries—entities that look terrifying but are actually hollow and weak. The interpreter translated it literally as "paper tiger," and this vivid metaphor immediately made headlines across Western media.

---

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage
This is a highly successful cultural export. The term "paper tiger" has been officially added to English dictionaries, functioning as a noun phrase. 

In modern English, it has transcended its original political context. You will frequently hear it used in business commentaries (to describe a corporation with fake prestige) or in sports (to describe a team that has famous players but performs terribly on the field). It perfectly describes anything that is outwardly threatening but inwardly powerless.
