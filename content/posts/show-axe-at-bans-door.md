## 📖 Origin Story

The story centers on Lu Ban, a legendary carpenter and inventor from the Spring and Autumn period (770–476 BCE) who is still worshipped today as the patron saint of Chinese builders. Lu Ban invented the saw, the carpenter's square, and the cloud ladder used in siege warfare. His skill was so extraordinary that ghosts and gods supposedly consulted him on construction projects.

One day, a young carpenter — confident in his own abilities — traveled to Lu Ban's village and began demonstrating his axe technique directly in front of Lu Ban's front door. He swung with enthusiasm. He showed off his grip, his stance, and his follow-through. A crowd gathered. The young carpenter beamed. Then Lu Ban stepped outside, picked up the same axe, and carved a bird so lifelike that it flew away.

The young carpenter disappeared from history. His name was not recorded. Lu Ban's name is still on temples.

## 🤓 Analysis & Usage

"Teaching fish to swim" suggests well-meaning redundancy. "Carrying coals to Newcastle" implies unnecessary commerce. Neither captures the specific arrogance of demonstrating a skill to the person who invented it. The young carpenter was not being helpful. He was being competitive — and catastrophically outmatched.

"Show axe at Ban's door" preserves the exact location of the humiliation. The axe was not demonstrated in a neutral field. It was demonstrated on the master's own doorstep, in his territory, under his roof's shadow. The location transforms a simple mistake into a declaration of war. It is the ancient Chinese equivalent of walking into Gordon Ramsay's kitchen and explaining how to boil water.

The Chinglish version also introduces a delightful occupational specificity. "Show axe" sounds like a lumberjack talent show. "At Ban's door" sounds like a delivery address. Combined, they create the image of a man arriving at a house with an axe, knocking politely, and then performing a series of aggressive woodcutting maneuvers on the porch. The homeowner opens the door. The homeowner is Paul Bunyan. The homeowner is not impressed.

## ✍️ Usage in Context

Modern Chinese deploy 班门弄斧 whenever someone overestimates their expertise in the presence of true mastery. It applies to interns correcting senior engineers, first-year students arguing with professors, and anyone who has ever explained Bitcoin to a cryptographer.

**Example Conversation:**

* **A:** "I told the sushi chef his rice was too warm."
* **B:** "How warm?"
* **A:** "Body temperature. I said proper sushi rice should be room temperature."
* **B:** "And?"
* **A:** "He has been making sushi for forty years. His father made sushi. His grandfather made sushi. I have been eating sushi for two years. I learned about room temperature from a YouTube video."
* **B:** "You showed your axe at Ban's door."
* **A:** "Ban came out. Ban showed me a grain of rice that he had been cultivating for three generations. It was room temperature. It was also perfect. I am never eating sushi again. I don't deserve it."
