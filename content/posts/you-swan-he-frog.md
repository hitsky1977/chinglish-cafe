## 📖 Origin Story
This brilliant piece of Chinglish is a multi-layered linguistic joke built on both phonetics and traditional Chinese animal metaphors:
1. **The Phonetic Pun:** In Chinese, "你算" (nǐ suàn - meaning "you calculate" or "who do you think you are") sounds strikingly similar to the English words **"You swan"** when spoken with a strong local accent.
2. **The Toad & Swan Imagery:** It draws heavily from the classic Chinese idiom *"癞蛤蟆想吃天鹅肉"* (A toad lusting after swan meat), which is used to mock someone who is overconfident, arrogant, or aiming completely out of their social/attractiveness tier. 

By merging the sound of "你算" into "You swan" and coupling it with the ultimate humble-brag counter-animal (the frog), this phrase was born.

## 😂 Why It’s Hilarious
To an English speaker hearing it for the first time, "You swan, he frog" sounds like a profound, cryptic line from a Zen koan or an avant-garde children's book. 

However, once you realize it's actually an aggressive internet roast used to put an overconfident person back in their place, the comedic effect is nuclear. It strips away all anger from an insult and replaces it with whimsical, animal-kingdom poetry.

## 🌐 Wikipedia Fame
Believe it or not, this linguistic masterpiece is so legendary that it has been formally documented on **[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_swan,_he_frog)**! It stands as a proud testament to the creativity of Chinese internet users, proving that world-class linguistic innovation sometimes happens one animal metaphor at a time.

## ✍️ Usage in Context
Modern netizens use this expression when someone is being wildly delusional about their status, capabilities, or chances with someone out of their league. 

**Example Conversation:**
* **A:** "I'm thinking of asking the CEO's daughter out on a date this weekend."
* **B:** "Bro, look in the mirror. You swan, he frog!"
